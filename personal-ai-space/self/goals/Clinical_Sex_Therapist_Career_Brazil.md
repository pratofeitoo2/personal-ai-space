# Working as a Clinical Sex Therapist in Brazil

## Education Requirements
- Bachelor's in Psychology (5 years) or Medicine (6 years for psychiatrists), accredited by CFP.
- Postgraduate specialization in Clinical Psychology or Sexology (master's/doctoral level).
- Supervised clinical training (300+ hours) in psychotherapy, including sexual health.
- Ongoing CFP professional development (40 hours/year).

## Certifications
- CFP registration mandatory for psychologists.
- AASECT Sex Therapist certification (international, adapted locally): Requires master's, 300 supervised hours, sexuality training.
- Brazilian titles like "Especialista em Saúde Sexual" from CFP or SBP.
- Annual recertification via CFP.

## Job Duties
- Assess and treat sexual dysfunctions, trauma, and relationship issues via CBT and counseling.
- Educate on sexual health; collaborate with medical professionals.
- Maintain ethical boundaries; work in private clinics, hospitals, or universities.
- Follow CFP guidelines for confidentiality and competence.

## Salary Expectations
- Average: R$4,000–R$8,000/month ($700–$1,400 USD); private practice up to R$12,000/month.
- Public sector (SUS): R$3,000–R$5,000/month.
- Higher in urban areas like São Paulo; bonuses for certifications.

## Challenges
- Cultural stigma around sexuality, influenced by conservatism and machismo.
- Regulatory gaps (no standalone sex therapy law); falls under general psychology.
- Emotional burnout, limited rural access, socioeconomic disparities.

## Ethical Considerations
- Governed by CFP Código de Ética: confidentiality, no dual relationships, cultural sensitivity.
- Informed consent, competence, and mandatory reporting for harm.
- Respect diverse identities (e.g., LGBTQ+); avoid exploitation.

## Cultural Norms
- Diverse: Urban areas more liberal (Carnival influences), but conservative elements (religious, gender roles) persist.
- High premarital sex acceptance, but stigma on dysfunctions or non-heteronormative identities.
- Therapists navigate family-oriented values and regional differences.

## Legal Regulations
- Psychologists regulated by CFP (Law 4119/1962); psychiatrists by CFM.
- Mandatory CFP licensing; disciplinary oversight.
- LGPD for data protection; Ministry of Health integrates sexual health into SUS.
- No specific sex therapy law, but psychotherapy guidelines apply.

## Career Outlook
- Growing demand due to mental health awareness, aging population, and chronic diseases.
- Job growth 15–20% in psychology; opportunities in telemedicine and holistic care.
- Expand programs at universities like USP; focus on niches for better prospects.
