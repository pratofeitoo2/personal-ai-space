**I. Framing Your Research:**

- **Define Key Terms:** Start by clearly defining "[[racism]]," "[[sexism]]," "[[homophobia]]," "[[classism]]" (and any other relevant axes of oppression you want to address) within the specific contexts of [[sexuality]], [[sexology]], [[mental health]], and [[psychotherapy]]. This requires a nuanced understanding of how these systems of power manifest in those fields. What does _sexist_ [[psychotherapy]] look like? In what ways is [[classism]] embedded in [[sexological research]]?
    
- **Theoretical Framework:** Ground your research in relevant theoretical frameworks. Consider:
    
    - **[[Intersectionality]]:** Understand how various forms of [[discrimination]] ([[race]], [[gender]], [[class]], [[sexuality]], etc.) intersect and create unique experiences of oppression for individuals. Kimberlé Crenshaw's work is fundamental here.
    - **[[Critical Race Theory (CRT)]]:** Examine how race and racism have shaped legal systems, social structures, and [[cultural norm]]s, impacting access to and experiences within sexuality, [[mental health]], and psychotherapy.
    - **[[Feminist Theory]]:** Analyze[[ power dynamics]] between [[genders]] and how [[sexism]] manifests in these fields.
    - **[[Queer Theory]]:** Deconstruct [[heteronormative]] assumptions and explore the experiences of [[LGBT]]Q+ individuals.
    - **[[Social Justice]] Frameworks:** Apply principles of equity, [[inclusion]], and [[empowerment]] to your research.
- **Establish Research Questions:** Develop focused and well-defined research questions that address the core issues you aim to investigate. Examples:
    
    - How do racial biases influence [[diagnosis]] and [[treatment]] of [[sexual dysfunctions]] in [[mental health]] settings?
    - In what ways does the lack of [[LGBT]]Q+ representation in[[ sex education]] materials contribute to [[homophobia]] and [[discrimination]]?
    - How do [[socioeconomic factors]] affect access to [[sexual and reproductive healthcare]]?
    - What are the [[ethical considerations]] involved in conducting [[sexological research]] with [[marginalized populations]]?

**II. Methodological Approaches:**

- **Literature Review:** Conduct a systematic and comprehensive literature review. Go beyond traditional databases to include grey literature (reports, policy documents, conference proceedings) and marginalized voices (blogs, community reports, activist writings). Identify gaps in existing research.
    
    - **Critical Appraisal:** Evaluate sources critically for biases, methodological limitations, and the perspectives represented.
- **Qualitative Research:** Qualitative methods are crucial for exploring complex social phenomena and understanding the lived experiences of marginalized individuals.
    
    - **In-depth Interviews:** Conduct interviews with individuals who have directly experienced [[discrimination]] in these fields. Use open-ended questions to allow participants to share their stories and perspectives in their own words. Focus on [[power dynamics]] in the research relationship and work to create safe spaces. Example: Someone from low income being charged high fee in [[therapy]] might feel bad based on financial reasons.
    - **Focus Groups:** Facilitate focus groups with diverse groups of individuals to explore shared experiences and perspectives.
    - **Ethnography:** Immerse yourself in a community or setting to observe and understand cultural practices and beliefs related to sexuality,[[ mental health]], and [[power hierarchies]].
    - **Participatory Action Research (PAR):** Engage community members as active partners in the research process, empowering them to identify problems, develop solutions, and implement change.
- **Quantitative Research:** Quantitative methods can be used to identify patterns, trends, and disparities.
    
    - **Surveys:** Administer surveys to large samples to collect data on attitudes, beliefs, and behaviors related to sexuality & mental health. Ensure the survey is sensitive and accessible to diverse populations.
    - **Statistical Analysis:** Analyze existing datasets (e.g., healthcare records, census data) to identify disparities related to race, gender, [[sexual orientation]], & [[socioeconomic status]].
    - **Experimental Studies:** Conduct experimental studies to examine the impact of interventions designed to reduce discrimination. Ensure the interventions are culturally appropriate and ethically sound.
- **Mixed Methods Research:** Combine qualitative and quantitative methods to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the research problem.
    
    - **Sequential Explanatory Design:** Begin with quantitative data to identify patterns and trends, then use qualitative data to explore and explain those patterns.
    - **Sequential Exploratory Design:** Begin with qualitative data to explore a phenomenon, then use quantitative data to measure the extent of the findings.
    - **Concurrent Triangulation Design:** Collect both qualitative and quantitative data simultaneously and integrate the findings at the interpretation stage.
- **Critical Discourse Analysis:** Analyze language, texts, and communication practices to identify how power relations are constructed and maintained in these fields.
    
    - Examine textbooks, journal articles, therapy transcripts, and media representations of sexuality, mental health, and marginalized groups.
- **Archival Research:** Examine historical documents, records, and artifacts to understand how discrimination has evolved over time.
