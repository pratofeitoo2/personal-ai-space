# Working as a Clinical Sex Therapist

## Education Requirements
- Bachelor's degree in psychology or related field (4 years).
- Master's degree in counseling, clinical psychology, or MFT (2-3 years), with clinical practicum.
- Specialized sex therapy training (1-2 years post-master's): 300+ hours in sexuality education and therapy skills.
- Post-graduate supervision: 2,000-4,000 clinical hours, including 300 supervised sex therapy hours.

## Certifications
- AASECT certification (gold standard): Requires master's degree, state licensure, 90 hours sexuality education, 60 hours therapy skills, 50 hours supervision, 300 clinical hours.
- Other options: American Board of Sexology or International Board.
- Renewal every 3 years with 30 CEUs.

## Job Duties
- Assess and treat sexual dysfunctions, trauma, intimacy issues via talk therapy.
- Use techniques like sensate focus, cognitive-behavioral methods.
- Educate on healthy sexuality; refer for medical issues.
- Work in private practice, clinics, hospitals, or specialized centers.
- Maintain ethical boundaries, confidentiality, and non-judgmentalism.

## Salary Expectations
- Median: $59,000-$96,000 (varies by role: counselors $59k, psychologists $96k).
- Private practice: Up to $100,000+.
- Growth: 15-18% through 2032.

## Challenges
- Stigma and misconceptions about the profession.
- Emotional toll from trauma stories; risk of burnout.
- Regulatory hurdles, cultural diversity, business aspects in private practice.

## Ethical Considerations
- Adhere to AASECT Code: confidentiality, no physical contact, cultural humility, informed consent.
- Avoid dual relationships; mandatory reporting for harm.

## Career Outlook
- Growing demand due to reduced stigma, telehealth, and awareness of sexual health.
- Opportunities in healthcare, education, corporate wellness.
- Focus on niches like LGBTQ+ or trauma for better prospects.
